Orbit is a map of the links you use most. Each sun is a group, its planets are links, and a planet's moons are related links. Everything runs in your browser on this computer, without an account.

Find and open links

Move around the Galaxy

  • Rest the pointer on a sun to see its first five planets. Rest it on a planet to see its moons.
  • Click a planet or moon to open it. Websites open in a new tab.
  • Click a sun to open its System, which shows all its planets. Planets that don't fit around the sun are listed below it. Use Return to Galaxy, or press Escape, to go back.
  • A group planet has no link of its own; it holds related links. Rest the pointer on it, or focus it with Tab, to see them. Click it or press Enter to keep them open; click elsewhere or press Escape to close them again.

Quick find

Type in Quick find in the top bar, or press / to jump there. Every word you type must match something about a link: its name, the sun or planet it sits in, its web address or computer name, its tags, or its note.

  • The sun or planet a link sits in narrows a search: “tech reviews” finds the Reviews moons in Tech.
  • Short forms work: “lab1 s3” finds lab1-server3. Small typos are forgiven: “engadgte” still finds Engadget.
  • Type #tag or tag:name to see only links with that tag.
  • Searching from a System takes you back to the Galaxy with the results.

The command palette

Press Ctrl+K (Cmd+K on a Mac) from anywhere. Type to search links and commands, such as Turn on Edit mode, Edit links, Show the Galaxy as a list, Switch to workspace or Run routine. Press Enter to run the highlighted entry.

Favorites and recent links

A row above the Galaxy shows your routines, favorites and the last ten links you opened. Star a planet or moon in Quick find results, or tick Favorite in its editor. Open all at the end of the row opens every favorite at once.

Open several links at once

  • Shift+click a planet to open it with all its moons. In a System, the Open a planet with all its moons buttons do the same.
  • Orbit asks before opening more than one tab, and leaves Remote Desktop links out.

Most browsers block every tab after the first until you allow pop-ups for Orbit. In Edge and Chrome, click the blocked pop-up icon at the right of the address bar. Orbit tells you how many were blocked.

Notes and placeholders

  • A planet or moon can have a short note (up to 500 characters), such as which account to use. It shows on hover and in search results, and Quick find matches it. Never put passwords in a note.
  • A web address can contain up to five placeholders, such as https://jira.example.com/browse/{ticket}. Opening the link asks for each value, filled in with the last ones you used. Open all and routines use the last values, and skip a link that has none yet.

Change how the Galaxy looks

All of these are in Settings, and are saved in this browser. See the Settings reference for every option.

  • Theme: Default, Red, Blue or Aurora.
  • Font and Font size: Inter or Orbit classic, from 90% to 130%.
  • Background: None, or Constellations: stars that drift, link up and gather around the pointer. Particles sets how strongly they're drawn.
  • Animations: Full, Calm or Off. Calm keeps the background still but lets it follow the pointer.
  • List view: the list button in the top bar shows every sun, planet and moon as a compact list. Links open the same way.
  • Always show moons shows moons around visible planets without hovering.
  • Show site icons adds each website's own icon to its planets and moons.
  • Sun icons: give a sun one or two characters, such as an emoji, in its editor. It appears above the sun's name.

Edit your map

Your changes are saved in this browser. Use Export → Whole map (JSON) in Edit links to keep a backup, and to move your map to another browser or computer.

Edit links

Open Settings → Edit links to see the whole map as a tree beside the editor for the selected item.

  • Use Add sun and Add a link at the top of the sun list.
  • Change a name, colour, description, sun icon, web address, Remote Desktop host, tags or note, then select Save.
  • The icons on each row edit, export, move up, move down or remove that item. Arrows and confirmed removals take effect at once.
  • In a planet or moon editor, the toolbar at the top has Save, Export, Move and Remove. Use Move to change the order, or to put the item in another sun or planet.
  • A sun's description can contain a link written as [label](https://example.com).
  • A planet or moon can have up to eight tags of letters, digits, spaces and hyphens. The sun list shows the tags in use; select one to search for it.

Edit straight from the map

  • Rest the pointer on a sun, planet or moon and click the pencil to open its editor, or press E on a focused item.
  • Use the trash button below the pencil to remove it. Undo removal brings back the last removed item and everything in it.

Add a link quickly

Paste a web address into Quick find and choose Add this link to your map, or choose Add a link in the command palette. Pick a sun to add it as a planet, or a planet to add it as a moon.

Group planets

  1. In Edit links, open a sun and choose Add planet.
  2. Set Kind to Group (no link), name it, and save.
  3. Use Add link for each website or Remote Desktop link in the group.

Change Kind to switch between a group and a linked planet; both keep their moons.

Rearrange by dragging

Turn on Edit mode in the top bar to rearrange the map with a mouse or pen. While it's on, planets and moons don't open; a sun still opens its System. Edit mode is off after every reload.

  • Drag a planet onto another sun to move it there with all its moons. Drag a moon onto another planet.
  • To set the order, drag onto an item of the same kind and choose Before or After.
  • Pause on a sun to show its planets, or on a planet to show its moons. In a System, a list of the other suns and planets appears while you drag.
  • Make a moon a planet: pause the moon on a sun's circle and hold on Make it a planet of until it fills, then release.
  • Make a planet a moon: drag a planet without moons onto another planet and hold on Make it a moon of.
  • Undo last drag reverses the most recent move.

Without dragging, use Move in an item's editor, including Make it a planet of… and Make it a moon of…. Touch dragging isn't supported, and team suns can't be dragged.

Import and export

  • Import a file accepts a whole map, a single sun, a planet to add as a new sun, or a bookmarks file exported from Chrome, Edge or Firefox. For a whole map, a review shows what each sun adds; Merge into my map adds what you're missing, and Replace my map takes the whole file.
  • Export → Whole map (JSON) downloads the open workspace's map with its routines and Remote Desktop presets.
  • The Export icon on a sun, planet or moon row saves just that item, for someone else to import.
  • Share favorites, and Share beside a routine, save a small link pack. Link pack under Import reads one.

Tidy up and undo

  • Find duplicates lists links that point to the same place.
  • Check links lists websites that don't answer. It needs Orbit started with python3 serve.py.
  • History keeps the map from before each of the last 20 changes; restore any of them.
  • Reset to file data replaces your browser edits with the project's map. History keeps the current one.
  • Orbit suggests a backup after 20 changes or 14 days without one.

Workspaces

A workspace is a separate map, such as Work and Home, with its own suns, favorites, routines, history and undo. Switch between them in the top bar or the command palette.

  • Orbit includes Main and the default Dev Toolbox workspace.
  • Add a workspace in Settings → Workspaces, starting from an empty map, a copy of the current one, or Main's default map. Rename or remove workspaces there too; Main always stays.
  • A removed default workspace stays removed until you add it back from its (default) option.
  • Settings and the team map are shared by every workspace. Export, import and Reset to file data work on the open workspace.

Routines

A routine is a named set of links you open together, such as the sites you start every morning with.

  1. Open Settings → Edit links → Routines → New routine.
  2. Name it, tick its planets and moons, and save.
  3. Click its chip above the Galaxy, or choose Run routine in the command palette, to open all its websites.

Tick Offer this routine when Orbit opens and choose a time and days. When you open Orbit after that time, a banner offers to open it. Browsers only open tabs after a click, so Orbit asks rather than opening them by itself.

Start your day with Orbit

  1. Make Orbit your browser's startup page. In Edge: Settings → Start, home, and new tabs → When Edge starts → Open these pages. In Chrome: Settings → On startup → Open a specific page or set of pages.
  2. Open a routine once, and allow pop-ups for Orbit when the browser asks.

Routines remember links by address, so renaming or moving a link keeps them working. Remote Desktop links are left out when a routine opens.

Remote Desktop

A planet or moon can be a Windows Remote Desktop link to a computer name or IP address, with an optional port such as lab1-server1:3390.

  • Download .rdp file (the default): clicking the link downloads a connection file. Open it to start Remote Desktop Connection.
  • Open mstsc.exe: clicking opens Remote Desktop Connection directly. This needs the Orbit Windows helper, installed once on that PC. Your browser may ask before opening it.

Install the Windows helper

From the Orbit folder, run this in Windows PowerShell 5.1, then choose Open mstsc.exe in Settings → Remote Desktop:

powershell.exe -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\windows\Install-OrbitRdp.ps1

If only PowerShell 7 is available, run the same command with pwsh.exe. Re-run it after updating Orbit. To remove it, run .\windows\Uninstall-OrbitRdp.ps1 the same way.

Saved connection settings

  • In a planet or moon's editor, Import .rdp file keeps a saved connection's settings with that link. Save the link afterwards.
  • Import many at once with Import .rdp files as planets on a sun, or as moons on a planet.
  • RDP presets save common settings, such as full screen on all monitors, for many links at once.

Orbit never stores passwords, tokens or signatures. Exports do include the other connection settings, so review them before sharing.

Team map

A team map is an Orbit map file that a group shares. Its suns appear first, marked TEAM, beside your own.

  • With the local server: python3 serve.py --team /path/to/team-links.json.
  • Opening index.html directly: python3 sync.py --team /path/to/team-links.json. Run python3 sync.py --no-team to remove it.
  • Team suns are read-only. Open one in Edit links to Copy it into your own map, or to hide it.
  • When the team file changes, a notice says what's new or removed.

Keyboard

/
Jump to Quick find. ↓ moves into the results, ↑ and ↓ move between them, and Enter opens one.
Ctrl+K
Open the command palette (Cmd+K on a Mac).
Tab
Move through the page. Focusing a sun shows its planets, and focusing a planet shows its moons.
Arrow keys
Move between suns, and between planets or moons. ↓ on a planet enters its moons; ↑ goes back out.
Enter
Open the focused link, or the focused sun's System.
E
Open the focused sun, planet or moon in its editor.
Shift+click
Open a planet with all its moons.
Escape
One step back at a time: hide a button tip, close the palette or menu, cancel a drag, turn Edit mode off, clear Quick find, then return from a System to the Galaxy.

Settings reference

Click the cog in the top bar to open Settings. The ? beside each heading opens its part of this guide. Settings are saved in this browser and shared by every workspace.

Edit links

Opens the full editor: the whole map as a tree, with tools to import, export, find duplicates, check links, manage routines and presets, and restore history. See Edit your map.

Active link map

Says whether you're seeing the map that came with Orbit or your own edits saved in this browser. Your edits take precedence until you use Reset to file data. Opening index.html directly and using serve.py keep separate browser storage; use export and import to move a map between them.

Look

Theme
Default, Red, Blue or Aurora.
Font
Inter (the default), or Orbit classic: DM Sans with Space Grotesk for labels and headings. Both ship with Orbit, so it looks the same on every computer.
Font size
Small (90%), Standard (100%), Large (115%) or Extra large (130%).
Background
None, or Constellations: stars that drift, link up and gather around the pointer.
Particles
How strongly the Constellations are drawn: Subtle, Normal or Bold.
Animations
Full (the default) animates the interface and the background. Calm holds the background still but lets it follow the pointer. Off stops animations, transitions and pointer effects. Your device's reduce-motion setting always keeps the background still.

Galaxy

Galaxy view
Orbits, or a compact List of every sun, planet and moon.
Always show moons
Shows moons around visible planets without hovering.
Show favorites and recent
The row of routines, favorites and recent links above the Galaxy.
Clear recent links
Empties the recent list.
Show site icons
Adds each website's own icon, loaded from that site. Off by default, so Orbit contacts no site until you open it.

Editing

Edit buttons on hover
Shows the pencil and trash buttons when you rest the pointer on a map item. E still opens the editor when they're off.
Button tips
When a button's tip appears: after 0.8 seconds (the default), after 1.5 seconds, or never.
Confirm before deleting
Asks before removing a sun, planet or moon. With it off, Undo removal is still available.
Backup reminders
Suggests exporting a backup after 20 changes or 14 days without one.

Workspaces and team

Workspace
Switch, rename or remove workspaces, and add a new one. See Workspaces.
Team map
Shows whether a team map is set up, and lets you show team suns you've hidden. See Team map.

Remote Desktop

Remote Desktop launch
Download .rdp file (the default), or Open mstsc.exe, which needs the Orbit Windows helper. The commands to install it are shown here. See Remote Desktop.
